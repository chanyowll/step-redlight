How the race works

  • 1
    Tap to run.

    The faster you tap, the faster your runner goes. About 3 taps a second holds a strong pace; 5 or more is a sprint. Stop tapping and they slow to a walk.

  • 2
    Stay under your threshold.

    Run above your threshold (the white line on your speed bar) and you burn stamina. The further over, the faster it goes. Run below it to recover. Your legs are cold for the first 800 m, so sprinting off the start line costs even more.

  • 3
    Build rhythm.

    Running steadily fills your Rhythm meter, and that raises your threshold from 12.5 up to 16 km/h. Big surges and walking break it. A steady start lets you run faster later.

  • 4
    Save your energy.

    Energy drains every step and never refills, and sprinting burns it much faster. Low energy lowers your top speed and stamina ceiling. If it runs out, you hit the wall and can only shuffle.

  • 5
    Tired legs slow down.

    When stamina is low, your runner slows down even if you keep tapping hard. Empty it and they collapse, then have to jog for a while to recover before they can speed up again. Three collapses and you do not finish (DNF).

  • 6
    Kick at the end.

    In the last kilometre the crowd carries you, so running over threshold costs less. Energy and stamina you saved pay off here: the more energy you have left, the faster you go when you tap all out and the less it costs, and the more stamina you have left, the faster your legs carry you. The winning plan is to start steady and get faster toward the finish.
